\section{National-Level Forensic Agency Operational Workflow}
For a national security or law enforcement forensic laboratory (e.g., National Forensics Agency), the recovery and authentication of an unknown field sample involves a rigorous, standardized three-stage workflow:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Field Swabbing and Chain of Custody}: Forensic investigators swab the suspect product surface (metallic casing, pharmaceutical seal, tax stamp, or post-blast residue) using sterile, nuclease-free polyester-tipped swabs moistened with $1\times\text{ TE}$ buffer or isopropanol. The sample is sealed in a tamper-evident evidence bag and assigned an encrypted digital accession barcode in DNAx Forensics\textsuperscript{\textcopyright}.
    \item \textbf{Chemical Etching and DNA Isolation}: The swab head is subjected to selective chemical etching to liberate encapsulated DNA from inorganic silica/ceramic shells, followed by silica column purification to remove PCR inhibitors.
    \item \textbf{Real-Time Multiplex qPCR Amplification}: The purified DNA is amplified in a 4-channel real-time PCR thermal cycler. The simultaneous optical signals across FAM, HEX, Texas Red, and Cy5 channels are deconvoluted by DNAx Forensics\textsuperscript{\textcopyright} to achieve 100\% mathematical identification against the central manufacturer database.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Chemical Etching Protocol for Encapsulated Taggants}
To recover DNA encapsulated inside dense silica ($\text{SiO}_2$) or ceramic shells without hydrolyzing the phosphodiester backbone of the DNA:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Silica Shell Dissolution}: The sample is treated with an ammonium fluoride/hydrofluoric acid buffer system ($\text{NH}_4\text{F}/\text{HF}$, $0.5\text{--}1.0\text{ M}$, buffered to $\text{pH } 5.5\text{--}6.0$ with ammonium hydroxide). Fluoride ions selectively attack silicon-oxygen bonds, converting the solid $\text{SiO}_2$ framework into soluble hexafluorosilicate complexes:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{SiO}_2 + 6\text{HF} \longrightarrow \text{H}_2\text{SiF}_6 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}
    \end{equation}
    The controlled pH ensures rapid silica dissolution within 10--15 minutes while preventing acid-catalyzed depurination of the DNA.
    \item \textbf{Surfactant Stripping}: Proteinase K and a mild chaotropic lysis buffer ($100\text{ mM NaCl}, 10\text{ mM Tris-HCl}, 25\text{ mM EDTA}, 0.5\%\text{ SDS}$) are added and incubated at $56^\circ\text{C}$ for 20 minutes to strip residual CTAB surfactant bilayers.
    \item \textbf{Column Chromatography}: The lysate is loaded onto a micro-spin silica membrane column in the presence of $3.0\text{ M}$ guanidine hydrochloride, washed twice with $80\%$ ethanol, and eluted in $25.0~\mu\text{L}$ of sterile nuclease-free water ($\text{ddH}_2\text{O}$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Significance for Forensic Identification}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Zero Optical Crosstalk}: The four emission wavelengths (FAM at $520\text{ nm}$, HEX at $556\text{ nm}$, Texas Red at $610\text{ nm}$, and Cy5 at $670\text{ nm}$) were completely resolved by the optical filter matrix, with no spectral bleed-through between neighboring channels.
    \item \textbf{Low Quantification Cycle Thresholds}: All $C_q$ values fell well within the valid forensic detection window ($C_q < 25.0$), demonstrating high copy-number recovery and high enzymatic efficiency ($E > 95\%$).
    \item \textbf{Conclusive 4-Probe Fingerprint}: The simultaneous positive detection of all four independent probes confirms the presence of the full contiguous 200~bp synthetic taggant, satisfying the stringent legal requirements for national forensic evidentiary proof.
\end{enumerate}
