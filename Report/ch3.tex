\section{Sequence Space \& Combinatorial Entropy}
For a linear DNA construct of arbitrary length $N$, with 4 canonical nucleotides ($\text{A, T, C, G}$) at each position, the total number of unique sequence combinations scales as $4^N$.

Taking the $N = 200\text{ base pairs}$ prototype synthesized in our proof-of-concept as an example, the theoretical sequence space $S_{\text{total}}$ is:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\text{total}} = 4^{200} = 2^{400} \approx \mathbf{2.58 \times 10^{120}\text{ combinations}}
\end{equation}
This sequence space ($2.58 \times 10^{120}$) is vastly larger than the estimated number of atoms in the observable universe ($\approx 10^{80}$), ensuring that no two randomly generated sequences will ever be identical.

\section{72-Probe Combinations}
In actual implementation, the central facility maintains a physical library of $M = 72$ pre-validated, orthogonal 24~bp DNA probes. By selecting $k = 4$ distinct probes per product batch, the total number of unique batch combinations $C(72, 4)$ is:
\begin{equation}
    C(72, 4) = \binom{72}{4} = \frac{72!}{4!(72-4)!} = \frac{72 \times 71 \times 70 \times 69}{4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} = \mathbf{1,028,790\text{ unique batch codes}}
\end{equation}

\section{Dosing \& Unit Economics}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Micro-Dosing}: DNA taggants are used at parts-per-billion (ppb) concentrations ($< 1\text{ ng/L}$). A standard $20~\mu\text{L}$ qPCR reaction requires only $\sim 10\text{ femtograms } (10^{-14}\text{ g})$ of DNA.
    \item \textbf{Batch Yield}: A single 1.0-Liter carrier formulation tags **over 100 million physical units** (bottles, cartons, or tax stamps).
    \item \textbf{Unit Cost}: The chemical reagent cost per tagged unit is **less than PKR 0.01 ($< 0.00004\text{ USD}$)**, making the system economically viable for mass deployment.
\end{itemize}
