\section{Sequence Space \& Combinatorial Entropy}
For a synthetic linear DNA construct of length $N$ nucleotides, with four canonical nitrogenous bases ($\text{Adenine, Thymine, Cytosine, Guanine}$) possible at each position, the theoretical combinatorial space $S_{\text{total}}$ scales as:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\text{total}} = 4^N = 2^{2N}
\end{equation}
Taking the $N = 200\text{ base pair}$ linear construct synthesized for this project as an illustrative prototype, the total sequence permutation space is:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\text{total}} = 4^{200} = 2^{400} \approx \mathbf{2.58 \times 10^{120}\text{ unique sequence combinations}}
\end{equation}
To put this astronomical number into perspective:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The total estimated number of elementary atoms in the observable universe is approximately $10^{80}$.
    \item The theoretical 200~bp sequence entropy ($2.58 \times 10^{120}$) exceeds universal atomic matter by **40 orders of magnitude**.
    \item Consequently, the probability of an adversary randomly synthesizing or guessing an authentic sequence is effectively zero ($P < 10^{-120}$).
\end{itemize}

\section{72-Probe Combinatorial Library Architecture}
In nationwide commercial deployment, synthesizing a unique, custom-made DNA strand from scratch for every single product batch would be financially and logistically prohibitive. 

To overcome this, the system uses a **combinatorial multi-probe mixing doctrine**:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The sovereign facility maintains a master physical library of $M = 72$ pre-validated, orthogonal 24~bp fluorescent probe targets.
    \item For any given production batch or authorized manufacturer, a unique combination of $k = 4$ distinct probes is drawn from the library and formulated together.
\end{enumerate}

The total number of distinct, non-interfering physical batch codes $C(M, k)$ generated is calculated via binomial combinations:
\begin{equation}
    C(72, 4) = \binom{72}{4} = \frac{72!}{4!(72-4)!} = \frac{72 \times 71 \times 70 \times 69}{4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} = \mathbf{1,028,790\text{ unique batch codes}}
\end{equation}

\section{Dosing \& Unit Economics}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Micro-Dosing Sensitivity}: A single real-time qPCR reaction requires only $\sim 10\text{ femtograms } (10^{-14}\text{ g})$ of DNA template for definitive fluorescence detection.
    \item \textbf{High Industrial Yield}: A single 1.0-Liter master batch formulated with $100~\mu\text{L}$ PCR product contains over $10^{13}$ copies of the construct, sufficient to tag **over 100 million physical commercial units** (vials, ampoules, carton labels, or tax banderoles).
    \item \textbf{Unit Cost Analysis}: The total chemical synthesis and buffer reagent cost per tagged product unit is **less than PKR 0.01 ($< 0.00004\text{ USD}$)**. This microscopic per-unit cost makes the system vastly more cost-effective than physical holograms, RFID inlays, or specialty luminescent dyes.
    \item \textbf{Environmental and Matrix Invariance}: Synthetic DNA is chemically non-reactive in petroleum, organic solvents, and pharmaceutical syrups, ensuring indefinite shelf life without altering the physical or chemical properties of the host product.
\end{enumerate}
